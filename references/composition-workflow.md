# Vietnamese Academic Composition Workflow

Writing is not sentence expansion. It is the controlled conversion of a research purpose, claims, and evidence into an inspectable discourse structure and then into Vietnamese prose.

## 1. Rhetorical brief

Record: discipline and subfield; genre; section; readers; communicative purpose; research question; central claim; supplied evidence; required length; terminology policy; citation convention; and unresolved inputs. If the requested text depends on missing evidence, mark the gap instead of filling it plausibly.

## 2. Claim-evidence ledger

Create one ledger entry per consequential claim using `schemas/claim-ledger.schema.json`. Distinguish observation, interpretation, methodological statement, literature claim, limitation, recommendation, and contribution. Record scope and stance before drafting.

## 3. Discourse architecture

Build a hierarchy rather than a list of topics:

```text
communicative purpose
-> central claim
-> supporting claims
-> evidence and warrants
-> qualifications or counterpositions
-> implication bounded by evidence
```

Reject an outline if sections merely name subjects, evidence has no claim, or a conclusion appears before its warrant.

## 4. Paragraph design

Assign one dominant function to each paragraph. Select only moves needed for that function:

- establish context or a precise problem;
- state a claim;
- present evidence;
- explain the warrant linking evidence to claim;
- contrast an alternative;
- qualify scope or uncertainty;
- derive a bounded implication;
- transition by carrying forward a concept, not by adding an empty connective.

Paragraphs need not follow a rigid template. They must have a recoverable function, controlled information progression, and explicit claim ownership.

## 5. Draft

Draft from the ledger and paragraph plan. Prefer clear predicates, stable terms, visible actors, and topic-to-new-information progression. Vary sentence form only when it serves information structure. Never use ornamental variation to rename a construct.

## 6. Adversarial review

Review the draft independently:

1. Can each empirical statement be traced to supplied evidence or an explicit citation anchor?
2. Does any sentence turn an inference into an observation or a recommendation into a requirement?
3. Does every paragraph perform its assigned role and advance the section purpose?
4. Are causal, contrastive, and consequential connectives warranted?
5. Could a reader identify the central claim and its limits without reconstructing the author's intention?

## 7. Revision order

Repair in this order: unsupported content -> claim-evidence mismatch -> architecture -> stance and scope -> paragraph cohesion -> sentence naturalness -> surface editing. Later repairs cannot override earlier constraints.
