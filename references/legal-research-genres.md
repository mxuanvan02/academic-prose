# Legal Research Genres

Structural playbooks for legal scholarship. Use with
[Genre playbooks](genre-playbooks.md) for the generic IMRAD move sets and with
[Rhetorical move registry](rhetorical-moves.md) for move identifiers.

Read this file before choosing a structure for any legal article, thesis
chapter, or law-adjacent policy paper.

## Why IMRAD Is Not The Default Here

IMRAD (Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion) is the reporting
convention of empirical research: it presupposes that the author generated
observations and must report them separately from their interpretation. Its
spread across disciplines is documented, not universal, and adoption varies by
field and journal (Oriokot et al. 2011, `10.1186/1756-0500-4-250`).

Doctrinal, normative, and jurisprudential work produces **arguments about
authoritative texts**, not observations of a sample. It therefore has no
`Results` section in the empirical sense. Forcing one produces a hollow
"Findings" heading that restates statutes, then a `Discussion` that carries the
entire argument. The failure mode is structural, not stylistic.

The governing test is not "does this follow IMRAD" but:

> Does the section order reproduce the inferential path from research question
> to warranted answer, given the method actually used?

Legal methodology has an explicit literature on this problem: whether doctrinal
scholarship has a method at all, and what it owes the reader by way of
transparency (Vranken 2012, `10.5553/rem/221225082012002002004`; van Gestel,
Micklitz & Rubin 2017, `10.1017/9781316442906`; Kestemont 2018,
`10.1017/9781839702389`; Taekema & van der Burg 2024, `10.4337/9781035307395`).

## Selecting A Genre

Route on the **object of study** and the **warrant** the argument relies on, not
on the topic.

| Object of study | Warrant | Genre |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authoritative texts: statutes, cases, doctrine | Correct interpretation and systematisation | Doctrinal |
| Two or more legal orders | Comparability established before comparison | Comparative |
| Law as it operates on actors and institutions | Observation, sampling, inference | Socio-legal / empirical |
| What the law ought to be | An explicit evaluative standard | Normative |
| Incentives, costs, allocative effects | An economic model | Law and economics |
| A regulated technology | Accurate technical premise plus regulatory theory | Law and technology |
| The body of prior scholarship itself | A pre-specified, reproducible search protocol | Systematic review |

A single article may combine genres. When it does, declare the primary genre and
subordinate the others; do not interleave two warrant structures inside one
section.

## Doctrinal

Sequence: `Introduction` -> `Legal framework` -> `Interpretation and analysis`
-> `Case law or comparative probe` -> `Evaluation` -> `Recommendations` ->
`Conclusion`.

`Analysis` occupies the structural position of `Results`. It is the load-bearing
section and must be the longest.

Doctrinal research is a systematic exposition of rules, principles and their
relationships, together with an assessment of doctrinal coherence and, where
warranted, prediction of future development (Hutchinson & Duncan 2012,
`10.21153/dlr2012vol17no1art70`).

Method transparency obligations, easy to omit and routinely challenged in
review:

1. Name the sources of law consulted and the cut-off date.
2. State the selection rule for cases. "Leading cases" is not a rule; a database,
   a court level, a date range, and an inclusion criterion are.
3. Separate settled interpretation from the author's proposed interpretation.
4. Report authority that resists the thesis before answering it.

Interdisciplinary extension is legitimate and does not convert the work into
empirical research; the doctrinal core retains its own method
(Hutchinson 2015, `10.5553/elr.000055`).

Failure signatures: a `Legal framework` section that paraphrases legislation
without an analytic question; recommendations that do not follow from any
identified defect; a claim of "gap in the law" with no interpretive argument that
existing rules fail to reach the case.

## Comparative

Sequence: `Introduction` -> `Method and selection of jurisdictions` ->
`Jurisdiction A` -> `Jurisdiction B` -> `Comparative analysis` -> `Lessons and
transferability` -> `Conclusion`.

The two country sections are inputs. The contribution lives in
`Comparative analysis`. Parallel national reports with no analytic section are a
descriptive survey, not comparative research.

Four requirements to state before the country sections:

1. **Purpose.** Understanding, harmonisation, unification, or law reform. The
   purpose determines what counts as a relevant similarity.
2. **Tertium comparationis.** The common reference point that makes the systems
   commensurable. Without it, comparison has no unit.
3. **Selection rationale.** Why these jurisdictions, and what makes them
   comparable for this question.
4. **Level of comparison.** Rule, institution, doctrine, or legal culture.

Method choice is itself a claim requiring justification
(Van Hoecke 2015, `10.5553/rem/.000010`). The functional method — comparing
solutions to a shared social problem rather than formally similar rules — is the
standard baseline and carries known limits, including the assumption that
functions are equivalent across systems (Michaels 2019,
`10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198810230.013.11`).

Transferability must be argued, not asserted. A foreign model working in its
origin system is evidence about that system. State the institutional,
procedural, and enforcement conditions the model depends on, then assess whether
the receiving system supplies them.

Failure signatures: no tertium comparationis; jurisdictions chosen by language
accessibility and presented as theoretically motivated; "lessons for X" that
restate the foreign rule as a recommendation.

## Socio-Legal And Empirical

Sequence: `Introduction` -> `Literature and theoretical framework` ->
`Methodology` -> `Findings` -> `Discussion` -> `Legal implications` ->
`Conclusion`.

This is the genre where IMRAD fits with least friction, because there are
genuine observations to report separately from their interpretation
(Baldwin & Davis 2005, `10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199248179.013.0039`).

Design and reporting obligations:

1. State the design and why it answers the question.
2. Report sampling frame, sample size, recruitment, and refusals.
3. Report the coding scheme, coder count, and disagreement handling for
   qualitative or case-coding work (Webley 2010,
   `10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199542475.013.0039`; Webley 2019,
   `10.4324/9780429952814-4`).
4. Report uncertainty. A proportion without a denominator is not a finding.
5. Keep causal language proportional to design. Observational data supports
   association; identification requires a design that isolates it
   (Epstein & King 2002, `10.2307/1600349`).

Legal training does not transfer to inferential practice: selecting confirming
cases is competent advocacy and defective inference. State how cases entered the
sample and what the sampling rule excludes.

Failure signatures: `Findings` that argue instead of report; interview quotes
selected to illustrate a conclusion with no account of contrary responses; N
reported once and never used as a denominator.

## Normative

Sequence: `Introduction` -> `Existing legal position` -> `Normative framework`
-> `Critical analysis` -> `Normative argument` -> `Reform proposals` ->
`Conclusion`.

`Normative framework` is the load-bearing section. The evaluative standard —
proportionality, rule of law, distributive fairness, human rights, efficiency —
must be stated, justified, and operationalised into criteria applied later.
Otherwise the critique reduces to the author's preference.

A theoretical or normative framework is a working instrument that must do
identifiable analytic work in the argument, not a literature-review ornament
(Taekema 2018, `10.5553/rem/.000031`).

Two separations to hold explicitly:

1. What the law **is** (descriptive) versus what it **ought to be**
   (prescriptive). Mark the transition.
2. The standard versus its application. State the standard before applying it,
   so a reader can reject the application while accepting the standard.

Failure signatures: "unreasonable" or "inadequate" without a stated criterion;
a framework section that names theorists but never returns; reform proposals
untraced to any evaluated defect.

## Law And Economics

Sequence: `Legal problem` -> `Economic framework` -> `Legal framework` ->
`Economic analysis` -> `Policy implications` -> `Conclusion`.

The question shifts from validity to consequence: what behaviour does the rule
price, and at what social cost. The deterrence tradition treats sanction
severity and detection probability as jointly determining compliance incentives
(Becker 1968, `10.1086/259394`).

Requirements:

1. State the behavioural model and its assumptions, including where actors are
   assumed rational and informed.
2. Distinguish an analytic prediction from an empirical finding. A model output
   is not evidence about the jurisdiction.
3. State the welfare criterion in use, and do not silently substitute one for
   another mid-argument.
4. Name the distributive consequences. Efficiency claims are not neutral.

Failure signatures: efficiency asserted with no cost accounting; a model
calibrated on foreign data applied to a domestic rule without stating the
transfer assumption.

## Law And Technology

Sequence: `Introduction` -> `Technological background` -> `Existing legal
framework` -> `Normative or theoretical framework` -> `Legal challenges` ->
`Comparative or empirical probe` -> `Regulatory options` -> `Conclusion`.

`Technological background` is a substantive section, not preamble: a reader who
misunderstands the technology cannot evaluate whether a rule reaches it. Keep it
factual and cited; do not smuggle the normative conclusion into the technical
description.

Two standing cautions:

1. **Novelty is a claim, not a premise.** Asserting that a technology defeats
   existing law is the argument to be made, not the starting point. Whether
   cyber-specific doctrine is warranted at all has been contested since the
   field's founding debate (Lessig 1999, `10.2307/1342331`).
2. **"Technology" is not a regulatory target.** Regulating an artefact, a
   practice, and a socio-technical system are different tasks with different
   instruments, and conflating them produces rules that miss or overreach
   (Moses 2013, `10.5235/17579961.5.1.1`).

Set out the regulatory environment before proposing an instrument: law is one
modality among design, market, and norms, and the choice among them is part of
the analysis (Brownsword, Scotford & Yeung 2019, `10.4324/9781351128186`).

Failure signatures: a technical section written from vendor material; "the law
cannot keep up" as an unexamined premise; a single instrument proposed with no
alternatives considered.

## Systematic Review Of Legal Scholarship

When the object of study is the literature itself, the protocol is the method
and must be pre-specified and reproducible: search strings per database,
date window, inclusion and exclusion criteria, screening stages with counts and
reasons for exclusion, and an extraction form (Page et al. 2021,
`10.1136/bmj.n71`).

Legal databases resist this: much authority is unindexed, national reports are
uncited, and full text is often paywalled. Report the retrieval outcome honestly
as a limitation. Sources whose full text was never obtained belong in a
"not retrieved" category and cannot be coded as included studies.

Failure signatures: "a systematic review" with no search string; counts that do
not reconcile across screening stages; conclusions drawn from titles and
abstracts while claiming full-text coding.

## Sections Common To All Legal Genres

### Title

Name the object, the jurisdiction or comparison, and the analytic angle.
"Pháp luật về trí tuệ nhân tạo" identifies a field, not a contribution.
"Liability for AI-generated content: a comparative analysis of the EU and
Vietnamese frameworks" identifies both.

### Abstract

`Background` -> `Gap` -> `Question` -> `Method` -> `Findings` ->
`Contribution`. In doctrinal work `Method` states the sources and selection
rule; `Findings` states the interpretive result. Claim nothing absent from the
body.

### Introduction

Territory -> evidenced gap -> delimited problem -> question -> preview of
contribution. In legal work, specify which kind of gap: a gap in the rules, in
their interpretation, in their application, in case law, or in scholarship.
These call for different arguments, and "few studies exist" is none of them.

### Research question as the spine

Every section must earn its place by advancing the answer. A history section
that does not bear on the question is length, not context.

## Vietnamese Legal Writing Notes

Terminology, once fixed in the glossary, stays fixed:
`quy phạm pháp luật`, `án lệ`, `khoảng trống pháp lý`,
`nghiên cứu pháp lý giáo điều` (doctrinal), `nghiên cứu chuẩn tắc` (normative),
`so sánh pháp luật` (comparative). Do not alternate with ornamental synonyms;
in legal prose a changed term reads as a changed concept.

Two recurring transfer errors:

1. **Method list without deployment.** "Bài viết sử dụng phương pháp phân tích,
   tổng hợp, so sánh" names techniques without saying where each is used or which
   sub-question it answers. State method against question.
2. **Method conflated with methodology.** The technique is the method; the
   justification for choosing it is the methodology. Both are required, and they
   are not the same sentence.

Citation form, heading case, and number format are owned by the target journal
or institutional template. See
[Academic Vietnamese standard](academic-vietnamese-standard.md) for the number
format rule, which is template-owned rather than voice-owned.
