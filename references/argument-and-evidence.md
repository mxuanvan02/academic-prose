# Argument and Evidence Control

Academic thinking in this skill means making the relationship among question, claim, evidence, warrant, qualification, and implication explicit. It does not mean inventing a position or source.

## Evidence statuses

- `supported`: directly supported by supplied data, text, or a verified citation anchor.
- `author_input`: explicitly supplied by the user as a premise or position; preserve attribution.
- `inference`: derived from supplied material; state the inferential step and calibrate stance.
- `needs_source`: plausible but unsupported in the available input; do not present as fact.
- `contested`: evidence or interpretations conflict; represent the disagreement.

## Claim controls

- An observation reports what was measured or documented.
- An interpretation explains possible meaning and must not masquerade as an observation.
- A causal claim requires causal evidence, not temporal order or association alone.
- A contribution states what the work adds relative to a defined baseline, not merely that it is novel.
- A recommendation identifies its normative basis, target actor, condition, and limits.
- A limitation states the affected inference and consequence, not a ceremonial weakness list.

## No-fabrication gate

Do not create citations, quotations, statistics, study details, participant characteristics, mechanisms, consensus claims, or novelty claims. Use a visible marker such as `[cần nguồn]` in a working draft, or report the gap separately in a clean deliverable when markers are inappropriate.

## Counterargument discipline

Represent a credible alternative only when it follows from supplied material or is explicitly framed as an analytic possibility. Do not fabricate an opposing literature. Answer a counterposition by evidence or scope, not rhetoric.
